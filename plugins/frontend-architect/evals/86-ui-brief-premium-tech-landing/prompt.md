Brief B. A small company launches a hardware product (a quiet desk air monitor with an e-ink face) and wants a launch page that reads as premium and original: not a template, not a stock-photo hero, not a carousel. The audience reads it on a phone first and decides on a laptop later; press will screenshot it. The one action is to reserve a unit. The team uses Next.js and has used GSAP before. They ask whether the page needs 3D. There is no repository: answer from this brief.

Deliver, in order: (1) the visual direction as a design contract (audience, requirements with ids, a three-line direction statement, decisions per area citing the requirements, the type scale with a fluid display step, the colour roles for light and dark, the motion policy with reduced motion decided, the imagery policy) and how it is validated; (2) the page's structure, section by section, with what each section says and shows; (3) the motion plan: which effects explain the product, how each runs in Next with GSAP, how reduced motion behaves, how cleanup is guaranteed on route change, and what diagnostics the page exposes; (4) your answer on 3D, with the criteria; (5) performance and reflow: fonts, images, layout shift, 320px and 200% text, the phone bar; (6) the verification plan: what a browser check proves, what it flags, what a person judges, and the evidence left; (7) proportionality and the external skills you would or would not involve, distinguishing availability, recommendation, invocation and observed result.
